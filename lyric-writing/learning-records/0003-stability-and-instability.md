# Learning Record 003: Stability & Instability

**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Lesson:** [003 — Stability & Instability](../lessons/0003-stability-and-instability.html)

## What was learned

- **Structural stability** is controlled by two levers: number of lines (even = stable, odd = unstable) and line lengths (matched = stable, mismatched = unstable).
- A great song alternates between unstable sections (verse, pre-chorus) and stable sections (chorus). The contrast creates a journey.
- Real-world examples analyzed:
  - **Billie Eilish — "when the party's over"**: 4-line verse (controlled) → 1-line pre-chorus (maximally unstable) → 2-line repeated chorus (maximally stable). The structural arc mirrors the emotional arc.
  - **Arctic Monkeys — "Do I Wanna Know?"**: sprawling verse lines (8–15 syllables, 11 lines) → clipped chorus lines (4–7 syllables, 9 lines). Both sections are unstable (odd counts) because the emotion is unresolved longing. Structure matches feeling.
  - **SZA — "Kill Bill"**: 3-line verse (unstable) → 4-line chorus (stable = "I've made up my mind") → 8-line bridge with repetitive matched lines, then a whispered line that detonates the pattern.
- AI lyrics fail structurally because they produce uniform 4-line sections with matched line lengths — no contrast, no journey.

## Skills practiced

- Counting lines per section and syllables per line.
- Identifying structural contrast (or lack of it) between verse and chorus.
- Restructuring a section by changing line count or length.
