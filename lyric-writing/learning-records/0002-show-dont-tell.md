# Learning Record 002: Show, Don't Tell

**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Lesson:** [002 — Show, Don't Tell](../lessons/0002-show-dont-tell.html)

## What was learned

- **Showing vs. telling** is the most immediately useful rewriting skill. "Telling" labels emotions ("I'm sad"). "Showing" evokes them through concrete, sensory details ("your smoke still lives inside my winter coat").
- The **filmmaker test**: if a camera can shoot it, it's concrete. If not, it's abstract.
- **Sense-mining** (object writing): start with an emotion, then explore what it looks, sounds, feels, smells, and tastes like. Generates raw material for concrete images.
- Real-world examples analyzed:
  - **The Killers — Mr. Brightside**: jealousy shown through actions ("she's calling a cab," "my stomach is sick") and enjambment ("touching his— / Chest now").
  - **Adele — Someone Like You**: mostly telling, but "summer haze" is the strongest image — one concrete detail anchors the whole song.
- Rule of thumb: aim for at least one concrete image per section (verse, chorus, bridge).

## Skills practiced

- Identifying telling vs. showing in existing lyrics.
- Rewriting exercise: 5 AI "telling" lines → concrete "showing" replacements.

## What's next

- **Stability & Instability** (Pattison Module 2): how number of lines and line lengths create motion or resolution. This is the structural lever — complementary to the content lever (showing/telling) learned here.
- The user should try sense-mining on their own before the next lesson.

## Zone of proximal development

The user can now diagnose telling vs. showing and has a technique (sense-mining) to generate concrete images. Next: structural tools — how the shape of a verse/chorus creates emotional motion, independent of the words themselves.
