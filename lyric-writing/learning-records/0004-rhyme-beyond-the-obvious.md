# Learning Record 004: Rhyme Beyond the Obvious

**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Lesson:** [004 — Rhyme Beyond the Obvious](../lessons/0004-rhyme-beyond-the-obvious.html)

## What was learned

- Rhyme is a **prosody tool**, not decoration. It controls how resolved or open a section feels.
- The **rhyme spectrum**: perfect rhyme (most stable) → family rhyme → additive/subtractive → assonance (open, searching) → consonance (dark, tense) → no rhyme (maximally unstable).
- **Rhyme schemes** as GPS: AABB = maximum resolution, ABAB = moderate motion, ABCB = common pop/rock, XAXA = very open.
- Guideline: loosen rhyme in verses (assonance, ABCB), tighten in choruses (perfect, AABB/ABAB), break pattern in bridges.
- Real-world examples analyzed:
  - **Amy Winehouse — "Back to Black"**: triple rhymes (AAA) in short lines, vowel shifts between groups, assonance loosening in the "knew/removed/through" group. Relentless, percussive, obsessive.
  - **Hozier — "Take Me to Church"**: loose assonance in verse opening (humour/funeral), tightening to perfect rhyme (bleak/week). Chorus barely rhymes — raw plea.
  - **Olivia Rodrigo — "drivers license"**: conversational assonance (about/house/around), bridge with almost no rhyme. Feels like unfiltered diary entry.
- When NOT to rhyme: if the emotion is too raw/urgent, forcing perfect rhyme makes it artificial. Trust images and structure.
