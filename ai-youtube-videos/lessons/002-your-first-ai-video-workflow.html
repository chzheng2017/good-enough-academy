Lesson 002: Your First AI Video Workflow

Lesson 2 of the series Estimated time: 45–60 minutes
Mission connection: Your goal is to publish your first AI-assisted video within 1–2 weeks. This lesson walks you through the exact workflow — step by step — from idea to published video. By the end, you'll have a repeatable process, not just a list of tools.

What Changed Since Lesson 1

Lesson 1 gave you the map — all 8 pipeline stages and the tools available at each one. Now you need to pick a route through the map and actually walk it. This lesson is that route: the minimum viable AI workflow that gets a real video published with the least friction.

We'll focus on the workflow for your specific channel concept: experimenting with AI tools in daily life, then explaining the tech behind them. That means screen recordings of real experiments, your voice narrating, and clear technical explanations — not fully AI-generated content.

The Minimum Viable AI Workflow

You don't need all 8 stages running on AI for your first video. Here are the 5 stages that give you the most speed with the least new tooling to learn:

1 Ideation: Generate & Validate Your Video Idea

Time: 15–20 minutes

Tool:

ChatGPT or Claude

What to do

  1. Brainstorm 10 ideas. Use this prompt (adapt to your interests): 
    I'm starting a YouTube channel about experimenting with AI tools in daily life,
then explaining the tech behind them. My audience is curious non-technical viewers.

Give me 10 specific video ideas. For each, include:
- A clickable title
- A one-sentence hook (why someone would click)
- Whether this is a "how-to," "experiment," or "explainer" format
  2. Pick your top 3. Choose based on: (a) which one you're most excited about, (b) which one you can film fastest, (c) which one has the most obvious visual demo.
  3. Validate with a second prompt: 
    For this video idea: "[YOUR CHOSEN TITLE]"

1. What would someone search on YouTube to find this video?
2. What are 3 competing videos already on YouTube about this topic?
3. What angle would make mine different from existing videos?
  4. Commit to one idea. Don't overthink it — your first video is about learning the workflow, not going viral.
Your channel's unfair advantage

Most AI tool videos are surface-level demos. You're an engineer — you can explain how the tool works under the hood. That's your differentiator. Pick an idea where you can show the AI experiment AND explain what's happening technically.

2 Scriptwriting: Structure Your Video with AI Assistance

Time: 30–45 minutes

Tool:

Claude or ChatGPT

The 3-pass scriptwriting process

Pass 1 — AI generates the outline. Use this prompt:

Write a YouTube video outline for: "[YOUR TITLE]"

Format:
- Hook (first 10 seconds — why should the viewer keep watching?)
- Problem/Context (30 seconds — what are we solving?)
- The Experiment (2-3 minutes — what I actually did, step by step)
- The Results (1-2 minutes — what happened, with screen recordings)
- How It Works (1-2 minutes — the tech explanation)
- Verdict & Call to Action (30 seconds)

Target length: 6-8 minutes
Tone: Conversational, curious, like explaining to a smart friend

Pass 2 — AI writes the first draft. Take the outline and prompt:

Now write the full script for this outline. Include:
- Exact words I'll say on camera
- [SCREEN RECORDING] markers where I should cut to a demo
- [B-ROLL] markers where I need supplementary footage

Write it conversationally — not like a blog post. Short sentences.
Don't use the phrase "in this video" or "without further ado."

Pass 3 — You rewrite for personality. This is the critical step that most people skip.

  • Read the AI draft out loud. Cut anything that sounds robotic or generic.
  • Add your personal reactions: "I was honestly surprised by this" or "This is where it gets interesting."
  • Add your engineer perspective: explain the why behind the tool, not just the what.
  • Cut 20–30% of the length. AI drafts are always too long.
The generic trap

If you skip Pass 3, your video will sound like every other AI-scripted YouTube video. Viewers notice. The AI draft is a starting point — your job is to make it sound like you.

3 Record: Capture Your Screen + Your Voice

Time: 30–60 minutes

Tools:

Descript Screen Recorder Your phone/camera

What to record

  1. Screen recording of your AI experiment. This is your primary content — the actual thing you did. Record your screen while you use the AI tool, narrating as you go. Descript has a built-in screen recorder, or use whatever you already have (OBS, QuickTime, etc.).
  2. Talking head intro and outro (optional for video 1). A quick on-camera intro builds trust faster, but it's not required for your first video. Screen recording + voiceover works fine.
  3. Don't aim for perfection. Record in one or two takes. Descript will handle filler word removal, audio cleanup, and retake cutting. The point is to capture raw material — polish comes in editing.
Recording tip

Record your experiment for real, including mistakes and surprises. Authentic reactions make better content than rehearsed demos. If the AI tool does something unexpected, keep rolling — that's your most interesting footage.

4 Edit: Text-Based Editing with Descript

Time: 30–60 minutes

Tool:

Descript

This is where Descript earns its place in the workflow. Instead of scrubbing a timeline, you edit by editing text.

The Descript editing workflow

  1. Import your recording. Drag in your screen recording and/or camera footage. Descript auto-transcribes it — this takes about 1 minute per 10 minutes of footage.
  2. Read the transcript and delete what you don't want. See a section where you rambled? Select those words and delete them — the video cuts automatically. This is the core "edit video like a document" experience.
  3. Remove filler words. One click: Descript finds and removes every "um," "uh," "you know," and "like." You review and approve. Source: descript.com
  4. Remove retakes. If you said something twice, Descript identifies the retakes and lets you cut all but the best version. Source: descript.com
  5. Apply Studio Sound. One click to remove background noise and enhance voice clarity. Turns a home recording into near-studio quality. Source: descript.com
  6. Add captions. Descript auto-generates captions from the transcript. Choose an animated caption style or use simple subtitles. Source: descript.com
  7. Export. Export as MP4 at up to 1080p (Free/Hobbyist) or 4K (Creator plan and above). Source: descript.com/pricing
The Underlord shortcut

Descript's AI assistant, Underlord, can do many of these steps in one shot. Tell it: "Make a tight cut of this recording. Remove filler words and retakes. Add captions." It handles the grunt work; you review the result. Underlord is available on all plans with varying limits. Source: descript.com

5 Thumbnail + Publish: Get It Live

Time: 20–30 minutes

Tools:

DALL-E / ChatGPT ChatGPT

Thumbnail (10 minutes)

  1. Generate 3 thumbnail backgrounds using DALL-E in ChatGPT: 
    Create a YouTube thumbnail background for a video about
"[YOUR TITLE]". Style: bold, colorful, tech-focused.
No text — I'll add text separately. 16:9 aspect ratio.
  2. Add text and your face. Use Canva (free) or Photoshop to overlay: a short title (3–5 words max), and optionally a photo of yourself with an expressive reaction. Thumbnails with faces get higher CTR.
  3. Pick the one that's most readable at phone size. Shrink it to the size of a postage stamp — can you still read the text and understand the image? That's how most people see it.

Title, Description & Tags (10 minutes)

Use ChatGPT to generate your metadata:

I'm publishing a YouTube video: "[YOUR TITLE]"

Generate:
1. Three alternative title options (keep the best hook)
2. A YouTube description (2-3 paragraphs, include a call to action)
3. Timestamps based on this script outline: [paste your outline]
4. 10 relevant tags/keywords
5. 3 relevant hashtags

Publish

Upload to YouTube. Set to public (not unlisted — commit to shipping). Your first video will not be perfect. That's fine. The goal is to complete the workflow once so you know where the bottlenecks are.

Ship it

The biggest risk isn't a bad video — it's never publishing one. Your first video teaches you more about your workflow than 10 more hours of planning. Get it live, then improve the process for video 2.

After Publishing: The Bonus Stage

+ Repurpose with OpusClip (15 minutes)

Tool:

OpusClip

Once your video is published on YouTube:

  1. Go to opus.pro and paste your YouTube URL.
  2. OpusClip's ClipAnything model analyzes your video — visual, audio, and sentiment cues — and generates 5–10 short clips automatically. Unlike older clipping tools that only work on talking-head podcasts, ClipAnything works on any genre including screen recordings and demos. Source: opus.pro
  3. Review the clips. OpusClip auto-adds animated captions and reframes to vertical (9:16) format using ReframeAnything with AI object tracking. Source: opus.pro
  4. Post the best 2–3 clips as YouTube Shorts, TikTok, or Instagram Reels.

Free tier: 60 minutes of video processing per month (refreshed monthly), plus a 7-day Pro trial with 90 minutes when you first sign up. That's enough for your first several videos. Source: opus.pro

Why this matters

Shorts drive discovery. They bring viewers to your channel who then watch your long-form content. One 8-minute video + 3 Shorts = 4 pieces of content from one recording session.

What You're Not Doing Yet (And That's Fine)

This minimum workflow intentionally skips some pipeline stages from Lesson 1:

  • AI voiceover (ElevenLabs) — You're using your real voice. More authentic for a personal channel. Revisit when you need multilingual versions or want to fix audio mistakes without re-recording.
  • AI video generation (Runway, InVideo) — Your content is real screen recordings, not generated footage. Revisit when you need B-roll you can't film.
  • AI avatars (HeyGen, Synthesia) — You're on camera. Revisit if you want to create content at scale without filming.
  • SEO optimization (VidIQ, TubeBuddy) — ChatGPT handles basic tags and descriptions for now. Add dedicated SEO tools once you have 5–10 videos and want to optimize for search.
  • AI music (Suno) — Background music is nice but not essential for your first video. Add it from video 2 onward.
